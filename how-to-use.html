01What it does

A matter listed under R.GANESAN when your register says E. Ganesh is invisible to a text search. Nobody appears. The order reads "None appears for the petitioner."

Callover reads today's cause list the way a very patient clerk would: it compares every name printed on it against your chamber's advocates, allowing for the ways a registry actually spells things — Krishnamurthy written as Krishnamoorthi, Lakshmi as Laxmi, a missing initial, a wrong one, two names run together, or a scanned page read badly. Then it tells you which matters are yours.

It also does three things a search box cannot:

  • It expands printed ranges. R.P.48 to 96/ 2023 is forty-nine matters, not one, and if any of them is yours you will be told.
  • It reads adjournment notices and removes the matters that will not be called, so you do not send a junior to an empty court.
  • When it is not sure, it asks — and puts everything it knows on the card so you can answer in two seconds.
Nothing is ever thrown away. A match it is unsure about becomes a question. A match it thinks is unlikely is hidden behind a toggle, not deleted. Showing you a row you did not need costs a glance; hiding one you did costs an appearance.

Type one name per line, or upload a file.

Enter everyone in the chamber, not just the arguing counsel

This is the single most useful thing you can do, and it is worth understanding why.

Chambers are printed together on a cause list. If two of your names appear on the same matter, that is powerful evidence in itself — so when the registry mangles one of them, the other rescues it. In the real Madras High Court list this was measured: E.SRIKANTH appears beside E.GANESH on two of his three matters. Your own roster becomes the thing that tells your Ganesh apart from the other ten on the page.

Write the names the way your firm writes them. Not the way you think the court might. Callover's whole job is to bridge the gap between the two — you do not need to guess at the registry's spelling.

If you upload a file

Callover works out which column holds the names, even when the heading is NAME OF THE ADVOCATE or Adv. Name or nothing at all. It shows you what it decided before you run anything, so you can correct it.

05Step 2 — your case register

This one is optional, and it changes everything.

Your register is your existing case list: diary numbers, case numbers, cause titles, which side you are on, the next stage. Upload it as .csv or Excel, in whatever shape it already has — Callover reads the columns it recognises and keeps every other column untouched so your exports come back with your own data still in them.

It does not have to look like anybody else's

Four columns are plenty, and the headings can be your own words:

Enough to work
Client Name, Case Number, Mobile Number, Cause Title
K.Jegan, R.P.66/2022, 9840920001, K.Jegan -Vs- J.C/E.O and others
  • Any headings. Party or Client, Case No or Case Number, Phone or Mobile, Title or Cause Title — all recognised.
  • Any order. Callover works out which column is which and shows you what it decided before you run.
  • The case number can be whole — R.P.66/2022 in one cell — or split across CaseType, CaseNo and Year. Both work.
  • Extra columns are welcome and are kept untouched. They come back in your exports exactly as you sent them.
  • Missing columns are fine. Anything absent simply shows as not in register on the cards, so you can see what could not be checked.
The one thing that must be complete is the case number's type. R.P.66/2022 matches; a bare 66/2022 cannot, because it might be an R.P. or an A.P. and Callover will not guess a forum to manufacture a match. If your register keeps the type in a separate column, that is fine — just include that column.

What it buys you

Without a registerWith one
Every question comes down to "does this name look like our advocate?" Most questions answer themselves, because Callover recognises your own case number on the list. You know your case numbers exactly; a name is only ever an approximation.
A matter where the counsel line is unreadable is missed entirely. It is still found — by its case number.
An adjournment notice, which carries no names at all, cannot be matched to you. It can. See section 8.
Registers go stale, and that failure is silent. New matters are filed every week. A six-week-old register quietly loses every recent filing, and the only symptom is fewer matches — which looks exactly like a quiet day. So Callover shows the age of your register permanently, warns you at fourteen days, warns you harder at forty-five, and carries that warning into anything you export. It will never refuse to run on an old register. It will just refuse to let you forget.

06Step 3 — the cause lists

Drag the PDFs in, or click to choose them. Add as many as you like, from as many courts as you like, in one go.

Getting the PDFs

There is a panel of links to the court portals. They open the court's own page in a new tab; you solve the picture-code there, download the list, and drop it in.

Callover never connects to a court itself, deliberately. The portals are captcha-gated, and a law firm's own software working around a government access control is not a risk worth taking. You fetch; it reads.

Three things on each file row

1

The kind of document. Callover detects whether it is a High Court list, a tribunal list or an adjournment notice, and shows you what it decided. You can override it. Only the reader you choose runs — pointing a High Court reader at a tribunal list produces confident nonsense rather than silence, which is far worse.

2

"I downloaded this from the official court portal". Tick it if you did. It is recorded in your results and exports as marked official by you — Callover cannot verify where a file came from and does not pretend to.

3

Thorough reading, which is on by default. Any page that does not read cleanly the first time gets read a second way. It is slower on difficult files and it catches more. Leave it on unless you are in a hurry.

07Running it

Press Run callover. A normal list takes a second or two. You will see pages counted as they are read.

If it is picture-reading, it will be slow — and you can always stop. A dense scanned page can take two minutes on its own. The progress panel tells you which page it is on and how far along it is, so a long wait never looks like a crash. And Stop and keep what has been read ends it immediately, keeps every page already read, and tells you which ones it skipped. Half a list is worth more than none.

08Adjourned — read this first

If you drop in an adjournment notice along with the cause list, this section is the most valuable thing on the page.

An adjournment notice carries case numbers and new dates and nothing else — no parties, no advocates, no cause titles. On its own it cannot tell you which rows are yours. Callover joins it to the cause list you loaded alongside it, and to your register, and recovers everything the notice leaves out:

What the join produces
notice    : R.P.541/2022  → reposted 15.09.2026
cause list: R.P.541/2022  E.R. Kannan through M/s.E.Ganesh
result    : Your matter R.P.541/2022 (E.R. Kannan) is NOT being
           heard today. Reposted to 15 September 2026 at 3.00 pm.
This is not a nicety. On the sample files supplied with Callover, 236 of the 240 matters on the 11 August list had been vacated by a notice issued the previous evening. A tool that reads only the cause list would have told that chamber to prepare bundles and send juniors for almost the whole day's board.

Adjourned matters appear in their own red-edged section, never in the attendance list, and never in the "matched" count. Each row shows the new date and time.

Watch for "date inferred — confirm". Adjournment notices print their dates in ruled table cells, often merged across several rows. Callover reads the actual drawn rectangles to work out which rows a date belongs to, which is the only way to get it right. If a file has no ruling at all, it will not guess silently — it marks the row and asks you. A guessed hearing date is indistinguishable from a correct one right up until the day it is wrong.

How to read that card

1

The green bar settles it. When the case number on the list is already in your register, the spelling of the name hardly matters — you are on record in that matter. If you see the green bar, you can usually answer without reading further.

2

The comparison table is the largest thing on the card because that is what the decision rests on. Case number first, because it is the most decisive; the advocate's name, the weakest signal, is deliberately not at the top.

3

Rows are shown even when there is nothing to compare. If you supplied no register, those rows still appear, marked absent — so you can see what Callover could not check, not only what it could.

4

The note is written in English, not in arithmetic. You will never see a confidence score on a confirm card: a number invites you to defer to it instead of reading the evidence in front of you.

5

The excerpt is verbatim from the PDF, with the matched text highlighted, so you can check the reading yourself without opening the file.

Answering quickly

Every answer is remembered. Once you have told Callover that R.GANESH is your E. Ganesh, it will never ask again — tomorrow, or next month. If you answer one wrongly, you can revoke it in Settings → Remembered spellings and the question comes back.

Two special cards worth recognising

"Your name is printed as a party to this matter, not as counsel on it." Sometimes a partner is a party in his own litigation. That is still something the chamber must attend to, so it is caught — but you are appearing and you are being sued are not the same thing, so it is never reported as an appearance.

"This page did not read cleanly." Your name was found on the page, but not where counsel names normally sit. It might be a listing that would otherwise have been missed; it might be your name inside a temple name or a party's father's name. Only you can tell, so Callover asks rather than guessing either way.

11Weak matches

Below the results there is a line like "▸ Show 4 weak matches — unlikely, but nothing is ever discarded". Click it and they appear, in exactly the same evidence-first layout.

These are the near-misses: J.GANESH when you are looking for E. Ganesh, or a fragment of a subject-matter line that happens to resemble a name. They are hidden by default so the questions that matter stay short — and kept, because occasionally one of them is yours.

Worth a look on a quiet day. If Callover reports far fewer matters than you expected, open the weak list before concluding that nothing was listed.

13Remembering your chamber

Retyping six names every morning is the kind of friction that ends a habit in a fortnight. So Callover can remember — on this computer, in this browser, and nowhere else.

Open Settings and tick Remember this chamber on this device. It stores your advocate list, your register, and the spellings you have confirmed. Next morning they are already there.

In SettingsWhat it does
Chamber nameShown at the top so a shared machine is obvious.
Export profileWrites one file you can carry on a stick or hand to a junior. This is how you set up a second machine.
Import profileReads that file back.
Remembered spellingsEvery question you have answered. Revoke any one and it will be asked again.
Keep a ledgerOff by default, and separate. It remembers case numbers and dates from previous runs so an adjournment notice can be identified even with no cause list loaded. It is the only thing that accumulates data you did not deliberately upload, which is why it has its own switch.
Erase everythingRemoves all of it, from this browser, immediately.

14Checking the privacy claim

Your register carries client names, case numbers and telephone numbers. You are entitled to be sceptical about putting it into a web page. So the claim is made in a form you can test rather than a form you must trust:

Turn off your internet and run it. It works exactly the same — which is only possible because nothing was ever being sent.

That is the whole verification. It takes ten seconds and it beats any badge or policy page. Underneath, the reasons it holds:

  • No upload. The browser opens your files from your own disk, the way a document opens on a desk. No copy goes anywhere.
  • No server. There is nothing at the other end to store anything.
  • No account, no sign-in, no email address. Nobody — including the authors — has any record that you used it.
  • No AI service. The matching is fixed, published rules, not a model and not an API. The same input always gives the same output, and you can be told exactly why any match was made.
  • Saved only if you ask. The chamber profile lives in this browser on this computer, and Settings will erase it.

The full statement sits at the foot of the app itself, below the export buttons.

15When something looks wrong

What you seeWhat to do
"None of your advocates appear in these lists." Note the page count in the same message. If pages were read, the list genuinely does not have you on it. Then open the weak matches to be sure.
Far fewer matters than expected Check the age of your register — matters filed since you uploaded it cannot be matched by case number. Check the Adjourned section: they may all have been vacated.
"N pages could not be read cleanly" Click through to see them and their raw text. Anything found on such a page is asked about rather than accepted. Often the file is a poor scan; try Thorough reading, or a better copy from the portal.
Advocates with odd names like a temple or a section number The document type is probably wrong. Change it on the file row and run again — that dropdown exists precisely for this.
Scanned pages not being read Picture-reading needs the page served over http rather than opened from a folder, and needs the vendor folder beside index.html. Callover will name the pages it could not read rather than failing quietly. Ask whoever set it up to host it, or run python -m http.server 8901 in the folder and open http://127.0.0.1:8901.
A run that seems stuck Look at the progress panel — if it names a page being picture-read, it is working, just slowly. Otherwise press Stop and keep what has been read. Nothing already read is lost.
A wrong "Yes" you want back Settings → Remembered spellings → Revoke. The question returns on the next run.
A case-number range flagged as too wide Callover refuses to expand an implausibly large range in case it has misread a page number, and tells you which one. Check that row by eye.

16A few words explained

CalloverThe court session where the bench goes through the list, calls each matter and fixes what happens next — which is what this does to your morning.
Cause listThe daily schedule of matters a court will hear.
Adjournment noticeA separate document vacating listed matters and giving them new dates.
CNRCase Number Record — a sixteen-character nationally unique case identifier.
CoramThe judge or judges sitting.
The dividerThe row of dashes on a High Court list separating petitioner's counsel above from respondent's counsel below. It is how Callover works out which side you are on.
Item numberYour position on the day's board.
"through"On tribunal lists the advocate is printed inside the party's cell, after this word — E.R. Kannan through M/s.E.Ganesh. Callover looks for it rather than for a counsel column, because many forums do not have one.
M/S.A chambers rather than an individual. Stripped before names are compared.
That is everything. Open index.html, put your names in, drop today's PDFs on it, and press Run.
